The wrong question

"What should we post this week?"

If you're looking for a copywriter to start writing before understanding your audience, what they care about, and what your competitors are saying, that's the first thing to rethink.

The better starting point

  • Who exactly are we talking to, and what do they already believe?I write for one specific reader, not "everyone".
  • Why should they choose us, and not the ten brands saying the same thing?I anchor every piece to one clear reason to choose you.
  • What are our customers actually saying, wanting, and objecting to?I build the copy from your customers' own words and objections.
  • What will still bring the right people to us a year from now?I prioritize content that keeps working long after it's published.
  • What should they do right after reading?I give every piece one clear next step.

Case study 01

You saved it for later. Later never came.

A launch film for an app whose whole promise is the part every bookmark tool ignores: coming back.

بعدين Baadein

A free Android app. Share a link from anywhere, pick a time, and it rings over the lock screen like an alarm. Arabic-first.

Consumer appEgypt · Egyptian ArabicLaunch film · Landscape videoHumor-led
My role
Research & VOC analysisStrategyScriptwritingCopywritingCreative direction

Course project on a real brand brief. Solo work, with mentor feedback folded into the final version.

The brand and the product

"Baadein" means later. The app exists for the sentence "I'll come back to this later". Every competitor stops at organising; Baadein is built for what happens after saving: the return.

The problem

People save links everywhere and almost never open them again. To them, Baadein reads as one more place to save, and they already have one that feels good enough. The film has to make the return, not the saving, the thing worth installing for.

The objective

App downloads. Make a problem-aware saver recognise the habit, understand the reminder in a single look, and install. Awareness is the route, not the goal.

Case study 01 · Persona & evidence09 / 19

Persona & evidence

The saver who never comes back.

Persona

Learns several things at once. Sees something useful, saves it with every intention of returning: a WhatsApp chat with themselves, saved posts, bookmarks, a sheet. Then it sinks under hundreds of other saves and is forgotten.

No name, no age. The interviews put them between 26 and 32, but nothing in the copy changes with that. The behaviour is the persona.

PainHunting for a link they know they saved. A course still on video one.
WantsTo actually use what they saved, in the free time they meant to use.
Objection"I already save in WhatsApp. Why another app?"

What the research said

بنسى اللينك حفظته فين، ولما برجعله بدور كتير علشان ألاقيه.
My watch-later playlist has 1.6k. Thousands of saved tabs.
I save everything "for later" to look at in the future.
بتاخد اللينك ترميه في شاتك، ومحدش بيفتحه في الآخر.
  • 3 meetings with people who have the problem
  • 39 VOC questions answered
  • 2 communities mined: Facebook and Reddit
  • 3 competitors read: Raindrop, Linkwarden, Readwise

Price barely came up. "I already have a place for this" came up every time. Competitors were read for one question: does anyone sell coming back? None do.

Case study 01 · Insight → Angle → Message10 / 19

The thinking

From a pattern to one sentence.

  1. Insight

    Saving gives a temporary feeling that the information is secured.

    So we relax, postpone the return, and forget it among everything else we saved. It's not a volume problem: twenty links behave exactly like two thousand.

  2. Angle

    Saving isn't the last step. The value starts when the content comes back to you at the time you need it.

    Baadein isn't a better place to put links. It solves the part after saving. That's the only angle where the product enters naturally instead of as "one more bookmark app".

  3. Main message

    It doesn't matter that the link is saved on your phone. What matters is that it reminds you, by itself, at the moment you need to go back.

Copy direction

The decisions before the words.

Funnel contextAwareness levelProblem aware. Part of the audience saves on autopilot and doesn't see a problem yet: they enter unaware and leave problem aware.
Funnel contextAction stageAttention. Cold audience, first contact with the brand.
Knowledge gapThey think saving is enough to come back. The missing piece isn't a better place to save, it's a trigger that returns them at a set time.

Creative direction · for the film

Everything you saved moved in with you.

One idea carries the whole film: the things you saved "for later" are people, and they've been living in your flat, waiting. A frame from scene 3, generated as visual direction for the shoot.

HookSaved content turned into house guests. You recognise the habit before anyone names it.
Format60 to 75 second landscape film for Facebook and YouTube. Egyptian Arabic dialogue, humor tied to the problem, one calm product ending.
WorldA crowded flat where every prop is a save: a printed post on the wall, a screenshot in a frame, a link on a fridge note.
Three looksThe dream: warm and busy. Waking up: quiet daylight. The product: the app's own colours, slow and plain.

Case study 01 · Results & reflection17 / 19

Results & reflection

Not launched yet. Here's how I'd know it worked.

A course project, so there's no dashboard to show. What I can show is the measurement plan, the first tests, and what the feedback round changed.

What I'd measure

  • Installs from the filmThe objective. Everything else explains this number.
  • Watch-through to the product partDo people stay past the dream and reach the demo at the end?
  • First reminder setActivation. Installing isn't the win. Setting a time is.
  • Cost per install by hookWhich opening earns attention cheapest.

What I'd test first

  • Opening with a hint vs. withoutDoes knowing "these are your saves" early lift watch-through?
  • 60s cut vs. 75s cutFewer characters, more product time.
  • PAS vs. Hook · StoryPain-led hook against objection-led hook, same audience.
  • Install CTA vs. comment CTATo prove, not assume, that the install ask converts better.

What the feedback round changed

  • The insight got sharperFrom "too many links" to "saving feels like doing". Twenty links behave like two thousand.
  • The objective got honestFrom awareness to installs. Awareness is the route.
  • The objection got real"How will it remind me?" was a question. "I already save in WhatsApp" is the objection.
  • The product got more screen timeThe joke can't be the only thing people remember.

How we'd work

Five steps, no surprises.

Not a contract. Just so you know what happens after the first message, and when the writing actually starts.

  1. 1One line, one callYou send one line about what you're building. We talk for twenty minutes: who it's for, and what the copy has to do.

  3. 3Direction, before writingAngle, main message, and what we won't say. One page, your sign-off. Nothing gets written before this.
  4. 4Copy, in roundsFirst drafts fast. Then two revision rounds, with your feedback consolidated in one place.
  5. 5Delivery and notesFinal copy in context, plus a short note on what to test first.
